Overview

Every Audience Sees Exactly What It Should

A fictional wealth firm has 3 branches, 6 advisors and 300 clients. Rather than building a separate extract for each audience, it keeps one governed dataset and lets policies decide who sees which rows, and in how much detail.

1

Advisor

Their own client book only, with enough contact detail to serve those clients.

2

Branch manager

Every book in the branch, with identifiers masked and net worth banded for oversight.

3

Compliance

All clients, unmasked, for surveillance, audit and regulatory requests.

4

Research partner

Aggregates only. Groups of fewer than 10 clients are suppressed, and no identifiers appear.

Prerequisites

Before You Begin

  • Enterprise Edition or higher for row access, masking, tag-based masking and aggregation policies.
  • A role to run it. ACCOUNTADMIN is simplest in a sandbox. For regulated environments, the top of Step 1 includes a least-privilege option: an administrator creates ENT_DEMO_ROLE once with only CREATE DATABASE, CREATE WAREHOUSE, CREATE ROLE and APPLY MASKING POLICY, and grants it to SYSADMIN. You then set demo_role to that role on the first line of the script.
  • One Snowsight worksheet, run top to bottom. The script creates its own ENT_DEMO_DB, ENT_WH and ENT_ roles and changes nothing else.
1
Step 1

Setup

Choose the role to run the guide, then create a dedicated database and an XSMALL warehouse that suspends after 60 seconds idle.

2
Step 2

One Role per Book, Branch and Function

Six advisor roles, three branch-manager roles, compliance and the external partner, all granted to the role running the script so you can switch between them. USE SECONDARY ROLES NONE makes each test role rely only on its own grants.

Many Snowsight users have secondary roles turned on by default. That makes privilege checks also use your other roles, so a missing grant wouldn't show up when you switch to a test role. Policies always evaluate CURRENT_ROLE(). The cleanup step restores the setting.

Step 3

Synthetic Clients, Accounts and Holdings

This step generates 300 clients, 600 accounts and about 3,850 positions in fictional tickers, deterministically, so every run matches. Every table carries ADVISOR_ID and BRANCH, so a single row access policy can protect all three without nested lookups.

Step 4

The Entitlements Mapping Table

Who may see what is stored as data. Each role maps to an advisor book, a branch or everything. Covering a colleague's book, onboarding an advisor or reorganizing branches becomes an INSERT or DELETE on this table. No policy needs rewriting or redeploying.

5
Step 5

A Row Access Policy Driven by Data

One policy reads the mapping table and is attached to all three tables. The same SELECT COUNT(*) returns 50, 100 or 300 clients depending on the role. At the end, one INSERT gives Advisor 01 temporary coverage of Advisor 02's book, and one DELETE takes it away again.

6
Step 6

Masking and Net-Worth Banding

Advisors get what they need to serve their own clients: full email and the last four digits of the tax ID. Branch managers get oversight detail only. Net worth is banded for everyone except compliance. A masking policy must return the column's own type, so each band shows as its lower bound: $250K, $1M, $5M or $10M.

7
Step 7

Tag-based Masking for New Columns

Attach a masking policy to a CLIENT_SENSITIVITY tag once. When a new column arrives, here a mobile phone number, tagging it protects it immediately with no new policy. The advisor still sees the number, and the branch manager doesn't.

New column, protected on arrival Real run output

8
Step 8

Aggregation Policy for the Research Partner

An aggregation policy on a one-row-per-client view means the partner can only run grouped queries. Any group with fewer than 10 clients is folded into a single remainder row, so no individual client can be singled out. Compliance is exempt.

Step 9

A Secure, De-identified Research View

The secure view exposes sector exposure by branch with no client, account or advisor identifiers, and it hides its own SQL definition. The partner can query it but can't read the underlying client table at all.

Step 10

Test as Each Role

One query for assets under management, run as three roles, returns three answers. There's no WHERE clause and no application logic. The firm's entitlement model is enforced the same way in every tool.

Step 11 · Optional

Share with a Partner Account

With a second Snowflake account, share the secure research view directly. The partner queries live data with no extracts or file transfers. Replace the partner account placeholder with the consumer's identifier.

12
Step 12

Clean Up

Uncomment and run this block. Dropping the database removes the tables, views, policies and tag. The warehouse and roles are dropped separately, and you can turn secondary roles back on if you normally use them.

Wrap-up

Key Takeaways

One governed dataset replaced four extracts, and every audience's access lives in policies and a mapping table you can audit.

Entitlements are data

A mapping table drives row access, so coverage changes are an INSERT, not a policy deployment.

Detail by purpose

Masking and banding give each role the level of detail its job needs, and no more.

Safe external analytics

An aggregation policy and a secure view let partners analyze trends without ever seeing a client.

Protected by default

Tag-based masking covers new sensitive columns the moment they're tagged.
